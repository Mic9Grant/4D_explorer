Six planes, not three axes

A 4D rotation turns in a plane, and there are six: xy xz xw yz yw zw. The three drag modes reach all of them. Only xz, yz and xy have three-dimensional counterparts — the ones touching w do not.

Nothing is really bending

Spin and tumble are rigid: the apparent inside-out writhing is perspective along w. The other thirty movements are real deformations — they move the vertices, so edge lengths genuinely change.

The contour is depth

Each edge is drawn as a shaded tube: dark rim, lit flank, specular line. Tube radius tracks w-proximity, so thickness is a fourth-coordinate readout, not decoration. Far geometry is drawn first.

Six grid planes, not three

3D software gives you one ground plane. 4D space has six coordinate 2-planes — xy xz xw yz yw zw — and any of them can carry a grid. Ticks run −2 to +2 in steps of 0.5. The grid rotates with the view, so it reads as the world frame while the object moves inside it.

The gizmo shows w

Four arms, filled dots for the positive ends and hollow ones for negative. Dot size is that axis's w-component: large means it is tilted toward you along the fourth axis, small means away. When an arm shrinks to a point it is aimed straight down w and has no screen direction left.
